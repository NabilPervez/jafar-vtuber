# Jafar — Neo-Baghdad Character Bible

Character profile, world bible, D&D 5e sheet, and story hooks. Live version: the "Jafar — Neo-Baghdad Bible" Claude doc. This file is a snapshot.

## 1. Character Profile

- **Name:** Jafar ibn al-Rih ("son of the wind" — a name he gave himself; his birth name was lost with his parents)
- **Age:** 29
- **Role:** Councillor of the Lower Wards on the Diwan (city council), merchant-prince, quiet power broker
- **Alignment:** Lawful Neutral, drifting toward whatever the city needs him to be
- **Signature colors:** deep crimson, gold, midnight indigo; his magic glows violet-magenta
- **Tagline:** *"Everyone in this city is selling something. I just read the price first."*

### Look
Lean, athletic build; sharp jaw, trimmed mustache and goatee, swept black hair. Crimson-and-gold sash stitched with his name in angular Kufic-style lettering, sleeveless indigo over-robe, white shirt, wide midnight trousers, leather sandals. Gold bracelets and rings on both hands — each one a focus engraved with micro-circuitry and sigils. Easy, knowing smile. He points when he talks, like he's closing a deal.

### Backstory
- **The Accident (age 7):** His parents, salvage workers in the Sabkha slums, died when an Aether-refinery pipeline ruptured. Qarun Dynamics paid nothing. Jafar was listed as "debris."
- **Street years (7–15):** Survived by fixing broken tech and trading scraps. Taught himself to read on stolen library data-slates, including fragments of a forbidden alchemical codex.
- **The Bazaar (15–21):** Built a stall into a trading network between alchemists and tech dealers who don't trust each other. Middlemen get robbed, so he learned to fight.
- **The Ascent (21–27):** Funded clinics, water filters and schools in the slums. The Lower Wards elected him to the Diwan — the first orphan of Sabkha to sit there.
- **Now (29):** He has proof Qarun Dynamics knew the pipeline would fail. Revenge or reform — he hasn't decided.

### Personality
- **Traits:** Charming, patient, always three moves ahead. Laughs easily, forgets nothing.
- **Ideal:** "Knowledge is the only inheritance no one can take from you."
- **Bond:** The people of Sabkha.
- **Flaw:** Believes he is the only one smart enough to fix things — and hides how much he wants the people who hurt him to fall.
- **Fear:** Becoming the kind of man who would list a child as "debris."
- **Moral tension:** A good man using a villain's toolkit. Does the toolkit change him?

### Abilities
Jafar is an **artificer**: every power he has, he built. Sigil geometry etched into scavenged tech, no implants.

- **Passive — Ledger of Debts:** Marks enemies who hurt his allies; his next hit on a marked target releases bonus lightning.
- **Q — Storm Gauntlet:** Right-hand gauntlet fires a straight-line bolt of sigil-lightning. Built from a junked arc-welder at sixteen.
- **W — Circle of Ilm:** Spinning geometric sigil shield that blocks projectiles, then bursts as a wind pulse.
- **E — Simoom Glide:** Hover-board dash that leaves a slowing trail of hot sand.
- **R — Wrath of the Wind:** Rises on the board and calls a lightning-laced sandstorm over a zone.
- **Social:** Persuasion, deception, reading micro-expressions, memorizing debts.

### Relationships
Jafar is **solo** — no sidekick, no love interest.

- **Sultana Nur al-Hayat:** Young reformist head of the Diwan. Ally and rival.
- **Malik Qarun:** CEO of Qarun Dynamics. Responsible for his parents' death.
- **Zahra:** Childhood friend from Sabkha, now a water-bender leading a Sandglass cell. Thinks Jafar sold out.
- **The Brass Mufti:** Ancient alchemist who taught him in secret. Possibly not entirely human anymore.

## 2. World Bible: Neo-Baghdad

### Overview
Year 2187. Neo-Baghdad rises from the rebuilt Round City on the Tigris — a megacity of 40 million. Neo-noir: rain that glows under neon, holo-calligraphy on skyscrapers, minarets that double as data spires, gold-tiled domes housing server farms. The Tigris runs in canals lit from below. Geometric girih patterns are everywhere — in architecture, on currency, in the magic itself.

### Paths to Power
A century ago, the **Unsealing**: scholars decoded the lost libraries of the House of Wisdom and found the old alchemists were right — the elements answer to the right geometry and will. Tech hit a new peak at the same time. Society split along a spectrum:

- **The Learned (Ahl al-Ilm):** Alchemists, chemists and elemental benders (fire, water, earth, wind, rare light and shadow). Power through study and sigil-circles.
- **The Wired (Ahl al-Ala):** Tech without implants or body modification — exo-gear, drones, smart fabrics, hard-light tools. Power through money.
- **The Fused (Al-Mumtazij):** Sigil-etched tech. Rare, expensive, often unstable. Jafar is quietly one of them.
- **The Unmarked:** Most people. No powers, little tech. The majority and the swing vote.

No cyberware or body-horror — the city's taboo is altering the body.

### Power Levels
1. **Spark** — a parlor trick, a basic drone
2. **Adept** — useful in a fight, licensed by the city
3. **Master** — can shift a street's weather or run a security grid
4. **Sage** — can reshape a district; fewer than fifty alive
5. **Legend** — myth-level; rumored to exist in the Diwan's vaults

### Districts
- **The Round City (Madinat al-Mudawwara):** Gold-domed center. The Diwan, the Grand Library, the elite.
- **Al-Karkh Spires:** Corporate towers of the Wired megacorps. Qarun Dynamics' HQ.
- **The Souk Infinite:** A fifteen-level vertical bazaar. Jafar's home turf.
- **Sabkha (the Salt Flats):** Slums on reclaimed riverbed. Where Jafar was born.
- **Bayt al-Hikma Quarter:** Academy district; floating libraries and alchemy guildhalls.
- **The Drowned Mosque:** Sunken ruin in the canals — sacred, haunted, black-market hub.

### Factions
- **The Diwan:** City council. Trade law and licensing of magic.
- **Qarun Dynamics:** Largest Wired megacorp; refines Aether, the city's fuel.
- **The Circle of Brass:** Old-guard alchemist guild that rations knowledge.
- **The Sandglass:** Underground benders and Unmarked who want power shared.
- **The Muhtasib Corps:** City enforcers with tech and sanctioned benders.

### Tone and Aesthetic
- Magenta, violet and teal neon against indigo night; warm gold interiors.
- Arabic architecture first — domes, minarets, muqarnas, mashrabiya screens that are also holo-displays.
- Magic circles: rotating geometric rings with calligraphy, spell circles crossed with girih tiles.
- Themes: knowledge vs. wealth, who gets to hold power, the price of climbing out.

## 4. Story Hooks
- **The Pipeline File:** Proof Qarun knew the refinery would fail. Expose it and crash the city's fuel supply, or use it as leverage?
- **The Sultana's Offer:** Nur offers him the vizier's seat — if he helps license all benders, including the Sandglass.
- **The Brass Mufti's Last Lesson:** His teacher vanishes, leaving a circle only Jafar can complete.
- **Zahra's Rebellion:** The Sandglass plans a strike on Al-Karkh. Jafar picks a side before the Muhtasib picks for him.
- **The Lamp:** A Legend-tier relic surfaces in the Souk. Everyone wants it. Jafar wants to make sure nobody gets it.

## 5. Notes on the Original Jafar
This Jafar keeps the archetype — clever, political, red-and-gold, a mind for power — but flips it: an origin story where the "vizier" is the self-made kid from the slums, and the question is whether he becomes the villain.
